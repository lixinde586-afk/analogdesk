## 1. The task

A trader is considering opening a position and wants to know, before committing capital, what the
historical record says about the state they are buying into. The task is deliberately the one the
sub-theme names - **decision stress testing** - and it has a fixed shape:

1. **Question in plain language.** A natural-language sentence, optionally in Chinese, naming an
   instrument and a holding period. No query DSL, no parameter form to learn.
2. **State description.** The engine computes 28 features for the decision session - the instrument's
   own tape, the benchmark, the rates and inflation complex, the crypto complex (because the
   reference market for a tokenized stock is a crypto venue), and the scheduled-event calendar - and
   standardises each on an expanding point-in-time window, so a z-score means the same thing in 2017
   and in 2026 and nothing downstream can see the future.
3. **Retrieval.** The 25 metric features are used to find the k nearest (symbol, session) pairs in a
   decade-long library, under an embargo that guarantees every reported outcome was fully realised
   before the decision date, plus two anti-clustering constraints so the "distribution" cannot
   collapse into one week of one crisis.
4. **Outcome distribution.** What those episodes actually did next: quantiles, tail probabilities,
   and the intra-path excursion, because an episode that finishes flat after a 20% drawdown was not
   a holdable trade.
5. **Calibration.** Raw analog percentiles are systematically over-confident, so a single global
   multiplier is fitted by split conformal prediction on 2019-2022, frozen, and never re-fitted on
   anything the trader is asking about.
6. **Stress suite.** The same engine, the same settings, re-run against named historical windows and
   against counterfactual shocks applied in z-space - each row carrying its own caveat.
7. **Actionable insight, and its limits.** A narrative that states what to anchor on, what could make
   the position un-holdable, and what the analysis cannot tell the trader - including where the
   engine's own validation shows it does not beat a naive benchmark.

The output is a decision input, not a decision. Nothing here sizes a position or recommends a side.

### [limits]

This analysis has concrete limits. The engine's own validation states: "Analog retrieval hits its coverage target out of sample and its width tracks each instrument's own volatility, but it is NOT sharper than a same-name unconditional band at matched coverage, and its raw PIT distribution fails a uniformity test. It is a stress-testing and provenance instrument, not an alpha source." The directional hit rate is 50.1%. At matched coverage, the analog conformal interval is 10.2% wide, while the unconditional same-name interval is 9.48% wide, meaning the retrieval adds -7.6% sharpness versus just using the name's own history. The conformal scale of 1.31 was fitted on 2019-2022 data and frozen; the 80.8% out-of-sample coverage is measured on 2023 onward over 2698 queries. Earnings dates for foreign private issuers come from 6-K full-text search and are noisier than domestic 8-K Item 2.02 dates.

Regarding the instrument you would actually trade: the wrapper status is "measured" on Gate.io spot v4 (public, keyless) as NVDAG_USDT. The premium band over the underlying's raw close is -0.44% to 0.65%, and the spread is 7.94 bps. The wrapper caveat applies: a fair tracker is not the underlying, and at 114 bp/day of tracking error the wrapper carries its own idiosyncratic risk; nothing here feeds the retrieval engine or the conformal scale.
